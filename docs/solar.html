Rooftop solar: is it worth it?

Indicative only. This is not a quote or financial advice. Get quotes and a shading check from an installer.

Each concept's roof was filled with panels. The model then traced the sun every hour of a typical year (PVGIS-ERA5 weather, latitude rounded to 21°N), allowing for shading from the house itself and from the assumed 3-storey neighbours. Panels that would produce less than 70 % of an ideal unshaded panel are left off. Change the numbers below to match your household.

How to read this

  • The limit is when you use the power, not how much the roof makes. Without a payment for surplus power, only the energy the house uses as it is generated saves money. A family that is out by day and runs the air-conditioning at night uses only part of it. A smaller system, or a battery, usually pays back faster than filling the roof.
  • Tiers help. Solar replaces the most expensive kWh first (the top EVN tier), so it is worth more to a heavy user.
  • The neighbours are assumed. Both side neighbours are modelled as 3-storey (11.2 m) blocks. If they are lower, output rises: see the "no neighbours" figure.
  • Shading means module-level electronics. Micro-inverters or optimisers are assumed (included in the cost), because the neighbours shade parts of the roof at different times of day.
  • Model: 440 Wp panels, 14 % system losses, panel temperature and glass-angle losses, results calibrated to PVGIS within 1 %. Payback allows for 0.5 %/yr panel ageing and 1 %/yr upkeep, with no price rises.
